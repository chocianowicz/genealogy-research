# Historical and administrative context (what changes where records are)

## Grand Duchy of Lithuania (to 1795)
- Units: voivodeship (województwo) → powiat; estates (dobra, włość, klucz) of the crown (ekonomie, starostwa),
  magnates, church (bishopric, chapters, monasteries), petty szlachta.
- **Wołoka reform (pomiara włóczna), 1557+**: land measured in włoki (~21 ha), villages in sznury; inventories
  list households by włoka/sznur with dues (czynsz, pańszczyzna, dziakło). Pomiara lists of ~1557–1620 are the
  earliest peasant name lists.
- **Inventories (inwentarze)**: made at lease, sale, transfer, death. Polish (from late XVII c.), earlier Ruthenian
  (old Belarusian Cyrillic). Give heads of households, sometimes sons/brothers, land, dues, deserted plots (pustki).
- **Social estates:** szlachta (incl. petty "zaściankowa/okoliczna"), boyars (putni, pancerni — military servants,
  often later claiming nobility), ziemianie, tax-paying peasants (ciągli), rent-paying (czynszowi), townsmen.
  "Boyars" in an inventory are not automatically szlachta.
- **Courts:** grodzki (castle) and ziemski (land) courts per powiat; books record sales, pledges, disputes, pedigrees.
  Lithuanian Metrica (Metryka Litewska) — chancery books.
- **Church records:** Catholic (Latin) from XVI–XVII c.; Uniate (Greek Catholic) XVII c.–1839; Orthodox; Jewish;
  Old Believers. Visitations list parish villages.

## Partitions
- 1772: Polotsk/Vitebsk/Mstislavl lands (east) → Russia.
- 1793: Minsk, central Belarus, right-bank Ukraine → Russia.
- 1795: Vilnius, Grodno, Brest, western lands → Russia (and Prussia/Austria in Poland proper).
- Consequence: the date a place became Russian decides when Russian revisions start there and which
  gubernia archive holds it. Administrative units were redrawn several times (1795–1802, 1843 etc.).

## Russian Empire
- **Szlachta verification (разбор шляхты)** 1795–1860s: nobility had to prove status before deputy assemblies;
  failures became odnodvortsy / "граждане" / peasants (big wave after 1831 and 1863). Sources: genealogical books
  of gubernia nobility, Heraldry decisions, dvoryanstvo files.
- **Revisions (ревизии):** 5th 1795–97 (first in most of Belarus/Lithuania), 6th 1811, 7th 1815–17, 8th 1833–35,
  9th 1850–51, 10th 1857–59. Filed by uezd → owner/estate (or state/treasury village, town, social group).
  Households numbered; men and (here usually) women; ages; changes since last revision.
- **1839**: Uniate church abolished — parishes and books become Orthodox. Look for pre-1839 Uniate books separately.
- **Confessional metric books**: from c. 1800 duplicates went to consistories → today in national archives.
- **1861** emancipation; ustavnye gramoty; volosts and rural communities; redemption acts.
- **1897** census (fragmentary survival). **1915** refugee wave (bezhentsy) east.
- Inventories of 1840s (инвентарные правила 1844–47) list peasant households of private estates.

## XX century
- 1921 Riga treaty: western Belarus/Lithuania (Wilno region) to Poland until 1939; eastern part BSSR.
- Polish records 1921–39: parish books, settlement (osadnicy), military, land registers.
- Soviet: collectivization, dekulakization deportations (1930–33), border-zone cleansings (1930s), 1940–41
  deportations of osadniki and others to the north/Kazakhstan, 1944–52 deportations; WWII partisans and losses.
  Use rehabilitation databases as leads; seek the card and file shelfmark.

## Practical consequences
- Find the **owner** of the village in each revision year to find the right revision volume.
- Find the **parish of each confession** for metric books; check the neighbouring parishes too.
- For pre-1795 work look for the **estate** (and its owner type — church estates have the best series).
